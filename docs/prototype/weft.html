Two replicas, a and b, both hold Hi and go offline. a types " there" after the i; b types "!" after the same i. On merge, i has two right children, a:3 and b:3, and siblings sort by id, so both screens read Hi there! rather than Hi! there. Whose sentence comes first is arbitrary; that both agree is not.

The editor is the easy part. The hard part is the data structure that makes “exactly the same document” a guarantee rather than a hope, and the protocol that gets a replica that has been dark for an hour back in step without re-sending the world.

  • A new item goes as the right child of its left neighbour.
  • If that seat is taken, it goes as the left child of its right neighbour.
  • Siblings on the same side sort by id. That is the whole algorithm.
No edit that has reached IndexedDB is ever lost, and no acknowledged edit is ever lost.
